# Literature refresh for the EVSP–V2G stochastic extension

Evidence checked **21 September 2026**. This is a decision-oriented, bounded review, not an exhaustive priority search. No solver, cluster job, or experimental result was changed. Primary papers, version records, author repositories, and an official forecast-data schema were inspected. A dedicated deep-research skill/tool was unavailable in the active tool catalog; the work used web search, primary-source reading, citation follow-up, and read-only repository inspection. Google Scholar access was attempted but unsuccessful; this report does **not** claim a completed Google Scholar search.

The strongest newly identified operational comparator is **Abdelwahed et al. (2025)**: it already evaluates rolling charging with uncertain solar, real forecast updates, fixed vehicle duties, and stationary storage. **Enyam et al. (2026)** is useful for the mechanics of scenario MPC, but assumes perfect PV foresight and allows charging only. **Caustur et al. (2026 revision)** adds a close deterministic PV–ESS–V2G comparison and a verified public dataset. These findings narrow the research claim further: neither adaptive bus charging under renewable uncertainty nor combining mobile and stationary storage is new by itself.

The worthwhile question is conditional and empirical: **when does causal V2G flexibility on mandatory duties change the preferred stationary-storage investment or restore service under constrained local supply, and does adaptive duty assignment add further value?** The submitted formulation and current gate findings make this a more defensible target than introducing another generic stochastic scheduling model.

**Forecast data lead, with a specific limitation.** The official [Elia historical PV API metadata](https://opendata.elia.be/api/explore/v2.1/catalog/datasets/ods032) was accessible and is saved as `sources/elia-ods032-metadata.json`. Regional generation also requires careful scaling before it represents local depot PV.

## Implications for the next experiment

These are recommendations for the local model, not results claimed by the cited papers.

1. **Keep the gate, but make the frontier the experiment.** The [independent assessment's 19 September addendum](../independent_review_20260916/ASSESSMENT.md) reports little perfect-information charging value with cheap BESS and substantially more without BESS. Those are local reported findings, not revalidated here. Sweep stationary-storage price and capacity, generator power/fuel limits, and truck availability around the boundary where adaptation matters. Report the BESS units chosen, incremental fleet cost, discharge energy, shortages, and expected cost together. A no-BESS result is economically interesting only if that regime is motivated, or if adaptive V2G moves the investment boundary.

2. **Separate four information/commitment comparisons at fixed assets.** Use (A) committed truck energy profiles evaluated with causal BESS/generator operation; (B) causal energy adaptation on the same complete route skeleton; (C) fixed-skeleton perfect-information adaptive energy dispatch; and (D) fixed-assets perfect-information duty/energy reoptimization. With identical objective, assets, initial/terminal states, service accounting and physical constraints, C is a lower cost bound on B. Thus **J(A causal) − J(C PI)** upper-bounds the attainable causal saving relative to A. **J(A PI) − J(C PI) does not provide that bound**: it measures only the benefit of relaxing truck-profile commitments within perfect-information models, and can be zero while causal adaptation has positive value. Likewise, C minus D is the duty-adaptation increment under perfect information; it does not establish the value of causal assignment changes. A comparison that also rebuys assets is an investment-and-information envelope, not the value of charging flexibility.

3. **Make a rolling-horizon baseline mandatory before new pricing.** Compare a simple deterministic forecast-based rolling LP/MILP with a shared-first-action scenario MPC and the proposed small-tree policy. Give all deployable policies the same observations, forecasts, assets, starting states, terminal rules and solve budgets. The rolling optimization may have an optimistic internal value function; its realized actions and realized cost must be evaluated on unrevealed trajectories. Enforce information consistency for trucks, BESS and generation, not trucks alone.

4. **Use realistic information arrival as an experimental axis.** A class label must be selected from information observed by its stated time. Compare day-ahead only, scheduled intraday updates, and perfect future information. Record forecast target time, issue time, input history and policy freeze date. A “cloudy/daylight class” computed from the whole realized day is future information even if the class itself is low-dimensional. An arbitrary last-observed-solar extrapolation is a legitimate baseline when labeled honestly, but not evidence of forecast value in a real data product.

5. **Strengthen the mobile-versus-stationary comparison.** Match energy and power limits, efficiencies, cycling charges and initial/terminal energy. Distinguish a purchase of extra vehicles for storage from using spare capacity in a transport-mandated fleet. Decompose the effects of availability windows, cycling costs and limited generation. Compare marginal BESS cost against marginal adaptive-mobile benefit under identical accounting; do not import grid-arbitrage savings into an isolated-grid fossil-cost objective.

6. **Adaptive duties are a separate model.** Fixed trip sets do not imply fixed vehicle assignments. Commit either the complete skeleton across all classes, or use class-indexed post-observation duty incidence and coverage rows. The distinction is already highlighted by Nath's scenario-dependent preprocessing. Independent suffix optimization is not an exact pricing algorithm for common post-observation duties unless the coupling is preserved. Verify tiny examples and reduced costs before claiming a complexity or exactness result.

7. **Do not spend the next iteration on a larger uncertainty inventory.** First retain solar uncertainty and mandatory duties, then test the frontier and one deployable charging policy. Additional unannounced tasks answer a different service-readiness question and should be modeled with a release time and explicit obligations. The absence of charging value in one cheap-BESS configuration neither proves that all adaptive duties are worthless nor justifies implementing them immediately.

**Contribution wording to test:** “We quantify how operational information and duty commitment alter the substitution between vehicle-to-grid flexibility and stationary storage under constrained isolated supply, and develop an optimization method for the resulting commitment structure.” This is a research hypothesis and positioning statement, not a first-ever claim. A positive finding needs a causal comparator and economic regime evidence; a robust negative result can identify when fixed profiles and ordinary storage already absorb weather uncertainty.

## Priority unresolved overlap: Zhuang and Liang (2021)

*Stochastic Energy Management of Electric Bus Charging Stations With Renewable Energy Integration and B2G Capabilities*, IEEE TSTE 12(2):1206–1216, DOI [10.1109/TSTE.2020.3039758](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSTE.2020.3039758), is a **high-priority overlap check before positioning the contribution**, not a low-priority citation. The [author’s institutional bibliography](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~hao2/) and [publisher-deposited Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/TSTE.2020.3039758) confirm the citation. The title directly overlaps stochastic bus energy management, renewables and B2G. The focused follow-up searched the exact title/DOI, Alberta author and repository domains, author/thesis terms, arXiv and GitHub; no accessible author full text or code was verified. The IEEE page yielded no usable body; Crossref supplies no abstract. The institutional bibliography links no paper PDF at this entry. Secondary abstract copies were discovery leads only, not accepted as formulation evidence.

**Still unresolved:** exact control and investment decisions; whether duties/vehicle availability are supplied or assigned; which disturbances are stochastic; observation timing and nonanticipativity; BESS versus bus dispatch; and code availability. Do not classify this paper as either a causal-PV predecessor or a perfect-information model until primary text resolves these points. This access gap limits the novelty assessment, not the proposed experiment’s usefulness.
